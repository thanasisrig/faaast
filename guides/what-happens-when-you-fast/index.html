What happens when you fast, hour by hour

Updated 22 October 2026 · By the Faaast team

What happens in your body when you fast, from your last meal to three days. These are the same stages Faaast shows on the timer, in plain words, and without the claims you’ll find in a lot of places online.

The stages at a glance

Hours since you ateStageWhat’s typically happening
0–4 hoursFedYou’re digesting your last meal. Blood sugar and insulin are up, and your body runs on the food you just ate.
4–12 hoursEarly fastingInsulin falls and your body starts drawing on glycogen, the sugar stored in your liver and muscles. Most people are asleep for a lot of this stretch.
12–18 hoursFat burningYour glycogen is running lower, so your body burns more fat for energy. This is where 16:8 ends. Hunger often comes in waves here and then settles.
18–24 hoursKetones risingYour liver starts turning fat into ketones, a fuel your body and brain can use instead of sugar.
24–48 hoursKetosisKetones are now a main fuel for your body and brain. This is where a 36-hour fast sits.
48–72 hoursExtendedDeeper ketosis. Keep drinking water and take electrolytes, like a pinch of salt in water. If you feel dizzy, faint or unwell, end your fast.
72 hours and moreProlongedFasting this long should be done with a doctor’s guidance. When you end it, start with a small meal.

These are typical timings, not a schedule. When each stage starts depends on what you last ate, how active you are, how long you’ve been fasting regularly, and your body. Research puts the switch from burning sugar to burning mostly fat somewhere between 12 and 36 hours, and measurable ketosis anywhere from 12 to 48. That’s why Faaast only uses the word ketosis from 24 hours.

Why the first 12 hours feel easy

For most of this stretch your body is still living off your last meal and its stored sugar. If you stop eating after dinner, you sleep through a lot of it. This is why 12:12 is a good place to start.

12 to 18 hours: where most people stop

16:8 and 18:6 both end in this stage. Your stored sugar is running lower, so your body leans more on fat. It’s also where hunger tends to peak. It usually comes in waves of 15 to 20 minutes, and water, black coffee or tea help it pass.

18 to 48 hours: ketones

Past about 18 hours your liver makes more ketones from fat. By the end of the first day they’re a main fuel. Longer fasts like 20:4, one meal a day (about 23 hours) and 36 hours reach into this stage. Drink plenty, and if you fast this long often, add electrolytes.

What about autophagy?

You’ll often read that autophagy, the body recycling old parts of its cells, “switches on” at 16, 24 or 48 hours. In humans that hasn’t been shown to happen at a particular hour, so we don’t put it on a timeline and Faaast doesn’t claim it.

Past 48 hours, take more care

Longer fasts aren’t for everyone. Keep up water and electrolytes, and end the fast if you feel dizzy, faint, shaky or unwell. Past 72 hours, do it with a doctor’s guidance, and break the fast with a small meal rather than a big one.

This guide is general information, not medical advice. Don’t fast without talking to a doctor if you’re pregnant or breastfeeding, under 18, have had an eating disorder, have diabetes, or take insulin or other medicine that lowers blood sugar.

Questions

When does fat burning start when fasting?

Your body burns more fat once its stored sugar runs low, typically from around 12 hours. It isn’t a switch at an exact hour: it depends on your last meal and how active you are.

How long does it take to get into ketosis?

Somewhere between 12 and 48 hours for most people. Faaast names it from 24 hours, when ketones are typically a main fuel.

Does autophagy start at 16 hours?

That claim is common online, but in humans autophagy hasn’t been shown to start at a particular hour. Faaast doesn’t claim it.

Is it safe to fast for 72 hours?

Fasts that long should be done with a doctor’s guidance. Drink water, take electrolytes, and end the fast if you feel dizzy, faint or unwell.
